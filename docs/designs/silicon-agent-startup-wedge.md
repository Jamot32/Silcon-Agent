## Problem Statement

Silicon Agent turns a plain-English spec for a small digital chip into Verilog, model-written tests, synthesis, a real sky130 layout and a GDS bundle. It was built for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon (submission deadline 2026-11-04).

The measured engineering evidence is mixed, and `docs/RELIABILITY_REPORT.md` states that the reliability mission is **not complete**:
- First held-out set G1: 62/90, missing the Tier 1, 2 and 4 targets. 48 of 50 app-DONE hardware deliveries were hidden-correct, and 2 were false success claims, retained in the record.
- Fresh held-out set G2: 81/90, with 66/66 deliveries hidden-correct. Tier 4 still missed its 100% target (15/18).
- DEV: 87/90.
- Diagnostic stress probes S1 (900 s deadline) and S1b (3,600 s deadline): 21/24 combined, 21/21 deliveries hidden-correct. These are diagnostic probes, not held-out measurements.

All of these sets, including their references, were authored within the project, and the measured route was Codex/gpt-6.1-sol, not Meta Muse Spark.

There are two open problems. Reliability still has gaps, and that work continues on its own track. Demand is completely untested. This session addressed the second problem and found no demand evidence yet.

## Premises

1. The hackathon submission and the startup question are separate tracks. Demo work produces no demand evidence, and the startup hypothesis does not get to change the hackathon audience.
2. The first-time student is a weak first startup customer, because free Tiny Tapeout CI and Wokwi exist and students rarely pay. Working engineers are the interview population.
3. "AI writes Verilog" on its own is unlikely to be a defensible wedge. ChipAgents does spec-to-Verilog/SystemVerilog plus testbench generation and debugging, and coverage reports about $134M in Series A funding by July 2026 (sources disagree on round labels) and 120+ deployments ([Converge Digest](https://convergedigest.com/chipagents-raises-21m-to-bring-agentic-ai-to-chip-design/), [VC Access](https://www.vcaonline.com/news/2026072902/chipagents-expands-series-a-funding-to-134-million-as-demand-grows-for-agentic-ai-in-semiconductor-design/), [Beinsure](https://beinsure.com/news/chipagents-raises-60mn/)). ChipAgents already touches verification, and EDA incumbents' AI verification offerings were not checked. The space between those offerings and this wedge is therefore unverified (Open Questions).
4. *(Revised after cross-model challenge.)* Verification evidence is the leading wedge hypothesis. Interviews have to confirm or kill it under the decision rule below. It is not yet a proven advantage or moat.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Ship the hackathon as planned, run 5 engineer interviews (chosen)
No new product code. The hackathon pitch keeps its audience and adds one accurately worded verification point (below). Run the Interview Plan in parallel. Effort S (human ~1 day plus calls; CC ~30 min of copy).

### Approach B: Bring-your-own-RTL acceptance report (next step, gated on the decision rule)
Scope is the Codex 48-hour cut: one design class, no layout, no ranking, no autonomous repair, and stop-and-ask clarification only (no interactive resume). Ambiguity handling follows one rule:
- **Blocking ambiguity** stops the run before tests are written and lists the open questions. An ambiguity is blocking when it changes an observable output: reset value or polarity, overflow or wrap, boundary or off-by-one behavior, or handshake timing.
- **Non-blocking ambiguity** lets the run continue, and the report lists every assumption the tests made. This covers anything that doesn't change an observable output, such as internal encoding or signal naming.

New components:
1. An RTL upload and validation path: `CreateRun` input, top and port discovery, lint and size limits.
2. A run mode that skips the RTL stage and reuses VERIFY's RTL isolation (`backend/app/agent/orchestrator.py:127` exposes only `test/` in VERIFY).
3. Stop-and-ask clarification output.
4. A report view.

Report categories, each backed only by named evidence:
- **Model-written tests passed or failed**, with the test file, simulation log and failing assertion.
- **Lint and synthesis clean**, with Verilator and Yosys logs.
- **Open assumptions**: non-blocking ambiguities the tests assumed. A run stopped on a blocking ambiguity reports only its open questions, with no test results.

The report never claims "correctness established." Correctness would need an independent check B does not have, such as a user-supplied reference model or formal equivalence, so that category is deferred.

Effort M (human ~1–2 weeks; CC ~2–3 days, with the clarification and upload path as the unestimated risk). Not chosen now, because building before interviews repeats the build-first pattern and endangers Nov 4.

## Interview Plan

- **Population and count:** 5 working hardware engineers, by **2026-11-18** (two weeks after submission). At least 1 before Nov 4.
- **Sources:** the named engineer and their referrals, plus a second channel that doesn't depend on referrals: former colleagues or classmates now in industry, and open-source hardware communities (Tiny Tapeout, FOSSi, r/FPGA) asking for working engineers.
- **Format and confidentiality:** the engineer walks through a past block from memory, a public or open-source block, or their workflow without showing source. No proprietary RTL, specs or screenshots are recorded or kept. Notes capture only role, workflow steps and tools (including any AI verification or debug tools), where trust broke, what they did and what it cost.
- **Questions to cover:** today's spec-to-sign-off workflow; whether and how they use LLMs for RTL; the last time they shipped or nearly shipped a wrong block; what that cost; whether they'd run a "bring your RTL, get blind-written tests plus open assumptions" report on a representative block; who would pay.

**Decision rule.** Start Approach B only if at least 3 of the 5 engineers each meet **all** of the following:
1. They describe a real trust gap with a measurable cost: hours of extra review, testbench writing, or a respin or near-miss.
2. They call their current remedy inadequate. Mature tools that handle it well don't count.
3. They would run the B report on a representative block.
4. They can name who would pay.

**Kill outcome:** if at least 3 of the 5 meet criterion 1 but fail criterion 2 (the trust gap is common and already well served), drop the verification wedge. Record what each engineer described as their biggest unserved pain.

**Shortfall outcome:** if fewer than 5 interviews are done by 2026-11-18, make no B decision. Extend once by two weeks using only the non-referral channel. If still short, treat it as a distribution signal and run office hours again.

**Default outcome:** any other result with 5 interviews done (for example, the trust gap is rare, or criteria 1 and 2 are met but 3 or 4 are not) means do not start B. Record each engineer's biggest unserved pain and which criteria failed, then run office hours again before building anything.

**Precedence:** evaluate the outcomes in this order and take the first that holds: Shortfall, then B, then Kill, then Default.

### R2-1 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Regression. The round-1 rule had a default outcome when the B threshold was not met ('If fewer than 3 do ... run office hours again'). The diff removed it and kept only B, Kill (trust gap common but well served) and Shortfall (fewer than 5 interviews). The Success Criteria require 'the B / kill / shortfall outcome', but in these cases none applies, so the decision can stall or be made by intuition.

**Remedy**

> Add an explicit default outcome for every result that is not B, Kill or Shortfall. For example: do not start B, record each engineer's biggest unserved pain and which criteria failed, and run office hours again before building anything.

### R2-2 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

> The Kill outcome drops the verification wedge when the trust gap is 'common' but well served, and 'common' has no threshold. The rule doesn't say how many engineers must meet criterion 1 and fail criterion 2. It also doesn't say what happens when the Kill pattern and a near-miss on B overlap (for example, 2 meet all four criteria and 3 meet criterion 1 but not 2). Dropping the wedge is a major decision, and the rule leaves it to judgment.

**Remedy**

> Define the Kill threshold numerically (for example, at least 3 of 5 meet criterion 1 and fail criterion 2), and say which outcome takes precedence when several conditions hold.

### R2-3 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> Approach B says the run stops on ambiguous semantics and lists open questions. But its report includes an 'Open assumptions' category defined as 'semantics the spec left ambiguous and the tests assumed', which only exists if the run continued and wrote tests on assumed semantics. The document doesn't say which ambiguities stop the run and which go ahead as recorded assumptions. So B's core behavior contradicts itself, and the next engineering review can't scope it.

**Remedy**

> Pick one behavior. Either the run always stops on ambiguity (then remove 'Open assumptions', or redefine it as the list of unanswered questions with no test results), or define a rule for which ambiguities block the run and which go ahead as assumptions named in the report.

### R2-4 — clarity (minor)

**Remedy**
